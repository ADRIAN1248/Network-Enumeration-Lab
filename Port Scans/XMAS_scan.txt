# TCP XMAS scan

## Objective
Identify open or filtered TCP ports on the target host (192.168.1.114) using nmap's TCP Xmas Scan Technique.

## Command used
nmap -sX -v 192.168.1.114

-sX: The scan sends TCP packets with the FIN, URG and PSH flags enabled.

A response interpretation of the scan:
- No response - Port is open or filtered
- RST response - Port is closed

## Analysis 
The RCP Xmas Scan produced different results compared to the TCP Connect and SYN scans.

The scan identified 23 ports as open|filtered (meaning the nmap could not determine whether the ports were open or blocked by a firewall)

Unlike a SYN scan, which receives a SYN/ACK response from the open ports, the Xmas scan relies on unusual TCP flag combinations to identify ports.

## Conclusion
The TCP Xmas scan successfully demonstrated a stealth scanning technique. However, because many ports were classified as open|filtered, further scanning techniques such as SYN scanning or service detection are required to confirm the actual state of the ports.
